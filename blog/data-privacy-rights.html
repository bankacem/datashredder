GDPR vs CCPA vs LGPD vs PDPL: Your Global Data Privacy Rights

Updated: July 31, 2026 · 8 min read

Data privacy law isn't one global rulebook — it's a patchwork of national and regional laws, and which one protects you depends on where you (or the company) are located. Here's how the four laws you'll encounter most often actually compare, and what right to deletion looks like under each.

GDPR (European Union)

The world's most influential privacy law. It gives you 8 rights, including erasure, and applies to any company worldwide that targets or monitors people in the EU/EEA — not just EU-based companies. We cover it in full detail in our complete GDPR guide.

CCPA / CPRA (California)

California's law gives residents the right to know what's collected, the right to delete it, the right to opt out of having their data sold, and the right to non-discrimination for exercising these rights. Unlike GDPR, it only applies to businesses that meet specific thresholds — generally over $25M in annual revenue, or that process data from 50,000+ consumers, or that earn a significant share of revenue from selling personal data. There is no single U.S. federal privacy law; a growing number of other states (Virginia, Colorado, and others) have their own similar laws.

LGPD (Brazil)

Brazil's Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados closely mirrors GDPR's structure but with local adaptations, including oversight by Brazil's National Data Protection Authority (ANPD). It grants a similar set of rights, including elimination of personal data processed with your consent.

PDPL (Saudi Arabia)

Saudi Arabia's Personal Data Protection Law grants a right to request erasure under Article 26 when data is no longer needed for its original purpose, consent is withdrawn, or the data was processed unlawfully — closely paralleling the GDPR grounds.

Other Notable Laws

  • UK GDPR — the UK's near-identical version of GDPR, enforced separately from the EU after Brexit.
  • PIPEDA (Canada) — Canada's federal private-sector privacy law.
  • PIPL (China) — China's comprehensive personal information protection law.
  • DPDP Act (India) — India's digital personal data protection framework.

Which Law Actually Protects You?

As a rule of thumb: the law that applies is usually based on where you are located when using the service, not your citizenship or where the company is headquartered.
