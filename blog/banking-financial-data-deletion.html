Why financial data plays by different rules

Ask a bank to delete your data and you'll usually get a polite explanation instead of a confirmation. That's not evasion — financial institutions operate under anti-money-laundering and tax retention laws that require them to keep certain records for a fixed number of years regardless of what GDPR or CCPA say about erasure. These retention laws override your deletion right for that specific category of data, and this is one of the more consistent exceptions across nearly every jurisdiction.

What you can actually request

The retention exception applies narrowly to transaction and identity-verification records, not to everything the bank holds about you. You can still request deletion of:

  • Marketing preferences and communication history unrelated to a transaction
  • Behavioral or analytics data collected through their app or website
  • Data from a closed application you never completed (e.g., a loan you didn't take out)
  • Old contact details that are no longer accurate, once you've provided updated ones

Retention periods vary by country

There's no single global number for how long a bank must keep your records. Under the EU's anti-money-laundering directives, member states generally require five years of KYC record retention after the end of a customer relationship, though several countries extend this to ten. In the US, banks typically follow similarly staggered requirements under the Bank Secrecy Act, often five years for most transaction records. The safest approach is to ask the bank directly what retention period applies to your specific account type, rather than assuming a single figure applies everywhere.

Credit bureaus are a separate problem

Even after a bank properly deletes what it's allowed to delete, credit bureaus (Experian, Equifax, TransUnion, and their regional equivalents) maintain their own independent records pulled from the bank's reporting — deleting data at the bank doesn't touch the bureau's copy. Correcting or removing bureau data requires a separate dispute process directly with the bureau, generally under fair credit reporting law rather than GDPR or CCPA erasure rights.

How to make the request correctly

Instead of asking for blanket "deletion," ask specifically for erasure of marketing and behavioral data, and a written explanation of what retention period applies to the records they're keeping. Frequently Asked Questions

Can my bank keep my data forever?

No — retention laws set a maximum period, typically five to ten years depending on the country and record type, after which the bank should no longer need to retain it for legal reasons.

Can I close my account and still have data deleted?

Closing the account doesn't trigger immediate deletion of everything — retained transaction records still follow the applicable retention period even after closure.

Does this apply to fintech apps too?

Yes. Fintech companies handling payments or lending are typically subject to the same anti-money-laundering retention rules as traditional banks.

Will deleting my marketing data affect my account or credit?

No, marketing and behavioral data deletion has no connection to your account status, credit records, or transaction history.
